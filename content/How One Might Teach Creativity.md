I've always thought about how one can [[Work and Care#^a8ef0f|teach creativity]].  It's something I obsesses about, since I do not like to agree that "some are just born with it".

I think that creativity boils down to three thing: [[Work and Care#^0c9099|care]], curiosity, and exposure. I do not have a better word for "exposure," but what I mean to say is that creative people have either seen or consumed a fair amount of work. Perhaps I should call this 'historical data' or 'experience'?

Okay... Moving away from semantics. What I want to describe is a framework that can be used to teach creativity.

I've had been doing this exercise where I take something, commonplace or otherwise, and try to think about alternative ways to design it. There are a set of standard questions one tries to answer while doing this exercise. 

- Why is it designed this way?
- What are the current design flaws?
- What tradeoffs might have made those flaws necessary?
- What biases introduced those flaws?
- Can it be made better?
- Is this the best form that fits its function(s)?
- etc.

Perhaps, it is not obvious, but the process of thinking about and attempting to answer these questions, helps one to practice the three things I mentioned earlier.

By trying to understand the history and context of something, one can develop care and curiosity, especially when trying to get into the mind of the original creator. What were they thinking? What didn't they see? What did they see correctly or incorrectly? What tradeoffs did they consider? Who where they thinking about while designing?

To create or design something, one needs to look at a fair amount of references. This is similar to what artists and designers call "research". You develop exposure by looking at what has been done in adjacent or tangential areas. I will stretch this further by saying one can develop "taste" in this way.

The framework I described might seem narrow, but the skills, memory, and knowledge that one develops by doing this, translates to almost anything else. Thinking of new ways to design something, and coming up with an alternative is a good way to build context about the world and also learn to solve problems.
