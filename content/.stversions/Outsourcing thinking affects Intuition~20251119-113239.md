The thing we lose when we outsource our thinking is good intuition. Good intuition can only be gained by struggling through a problem or dilemma. The path to a solving a problem is often non-linear and filled with obstacles that provide more information/context about said problem. Sometimes, it could even be unrelated information that can become relevant later. 

Another important things is what I like to call "cursed knowledge." This is the knowledge you gain by working on and failing at something. The reason it's cursed is that this information/knowledge can only be gained through failure.

Good intuition can be many things;  a heuristic for how things should work, a good guess about how to solve something, a good feel for what words and wouldn't. Our intuition becomes better with context and information, which can only be gained by trying and failing multiple times. This is why the "arena" concept is popular amongst high agency people. True learning can onlythe arena.
